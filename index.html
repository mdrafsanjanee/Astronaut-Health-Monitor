Health monitoring for astronauts on long-duration missions

A working prototype that watches an astronaut's vitals, sleep, exercise, wellbeing, and musculoskeletal risk alongside the spacecraft's environment — detects concerning trends before they become emergencies — and connects the crew to Mission Control with a clear monitor → detect → explain → act → record workflow.

HTML · CSS · JS no framework, no build step
3 crew simulated, 45-day demo mission
NASA-STD-3001 sourced environmental & health limits

The workflow

MONITOR→ DETECT→ EXPLAIN→ ACT→ RECORD

All telemetry in this prototype is simulated — generated from published NASA research and standards, not real flight data. Every threshold the alert engine uses is defined in data/nasa-references.json and cited below, so it's clear which numbers come from an official NASA standard and which are general reference values chosen for the demo.

Primary data sources

  • NASA-STD-3001, Volume 1 & Volume 2 (Space Flight Human System Standard) — habitable atmosphere, exercise, and health-monitoring requirements.
  • NASA OCHMO Technical Briefs — OCHMO-TB-002 (ECLSS), OCHMO-TB-003 (Habitable Atmosphere), OCHMO-TB-004 (Carbon Dioxide), OCHMO-TB-030 (Bone Loss).
  • Barger LK, et al. "Prevalence of sleep deficiency and use of hypnotic drugs in astronauts before, during, and after spaceflight." Lancet Neurology, 2014.
  • Smith SM, et al. bone-loss & exercise-compliance findings, ISS 2006-2009, and NASA/TM-2018-219938 on modeled bone mineral density change.
  • Ploutz-Snyder L. "An Evidence-Based Approach to Exercise Prescriptions on ISS," NASA Johnson Space Center.
