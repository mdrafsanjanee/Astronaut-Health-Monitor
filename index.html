The workflow

MONITOR→ DETECT→ EXPLAIN→ ACT→ RECORD

Apollo 15's own history walks through every step of this loop for real: Irwin's EKG showed an abnormal rhythm (monitor), flight surgeon Dr. Charles Berry recognized it as serious (detect), leadership weighed what it meant for the mission (explain), Mission Control made a real call about what to do — and what to tell the crew (act) — and it's still documented and studied today (record). Two corrections worth knowing before you dig in are in the README's "Corrections" section.

Primary data sources

  • Apollo 15 Mission Report (MSC-07230), NASA, 1971 — Section 10, Biomedical Evaluation.
  • Apollo 15 Activities Timeline, Table 4-I — second-accurate EVA and liftoff timestamps (an.rsl.wustl.edu).

  • Delp MD, et al., arXiv:2208.00892 — the specific PVC count and GET for Irwin's arrhythmia.
  • NASA SP-368, Biomedical Results of Apollo — Environmental Control System design (cabin atmosphere, CO2 removal).
